## Highlight: 2nd Cree Nation Business Summit (August 2025)

CREE NATION BUSINESS SUMMIT
August 19–21, 2025 – Mistissini, Eeyou Istchee

“Strong connections build strong economies — and the
Cree Nation Business Summit was only the beginning.”
— 2025 Cree Nation Business Summit Report

The 2nd Cree Nation Business Summit brought
together more than 200 attendees in Mistissini for
three days of connection, learning, and economic
vision-building. Held at the Neoskweskau Sports
Complex under the theme Eeyou Entrepreneurship in
Focus, the Summit united youth, entrepreneurs, and
leaders to strengthen the Eeyou economy together.
The event featured three signature programs. The
Eeyou Istchee Bear Den Case Study Competition,
organized in partnership with Eeyoupreneur, guided
40 youth through the full entrepreneurial process —
brainstorming, business planning, promotional video
production, and a final pitch before a panel of judges.
Teams developed seven unique business ideas, with
Clearwater Plaza (Team 3) taking first place, Eeyou
and Me Tourism (Team 6) second, and Big River
Eeyou Centre (Team 2) third.
The Business and Leader Exchange Days drew
60 entrepreneurs and 25 Cree organizations and
leaders for roundtables, panels, workshops, and
networking. Two roundtable discussions — on the

creation of a Cree Nation Business Association and
the development of a Local Administration Toolkit —
surfaced key priorities including capacity building,
access to capital, fair contracting, and centralized
business information.
The Summit concluded with the inaugural Eeyou
Istchee Regional Business Awards Gala, honouring
five foundational businesses ahead of the JBNQA’s
50th Anniversary: A.D.E.L.S. Restaurant (1971),
Blackned Construction (1975), Waswanipi Grocery
Store (1978/79), Meechum Reg’d (1982), and Natives
Exploration Services (1984).
Presented by the CNG Department of Commerce
and Industry, the Summit reinvested over $330,000
into the Cree economy and laid a strong foundation
for future partnerships. Planning is already underway

ORGANIZATIONAL OVERVIEW
Our Mandate
The Department of Commerce and Industry is mandated to
diversify and stimulate economic growth in Eeyou Istchee
by supporting Cree entrepreneurs, community-based enterprises, and regional economic initiatives.

Mission
The mission of the Department of Commerce and Industry
is to support and advance economic development in Eeyou
Istchee through strategic program delivery, sectoral development, and economic governance tools that enable Cree
individuals, communities, and enterprises to participate
meaningfully in the regional economy.

Vision
The Department of Commerce and Industry envisions a
strong, diversified, and self determined Cree economy in
which Eeyouch and Eenouch benefit from long term prosperity rooted in Cree values, stewardship of the land, and
community well being.

Our Pillars
The DCI is driven by 3 economic pillars:
1. Local Enterprises
2. Natural Resource Development
3. Cooperative and Sustainable Economies

Site Visit – Project Windfall
In early September, the Sectoral team had the opportunity to visit Project Windfall, located within Waswanipi
territory. The team traveled to Lebel-sur-Quévillon before
continuing on to the site. During the visit, the team met
with the community and Gold Fields representatives on
site. In the afternoon, the team toured the exploration
camps and later accessed the underground ramp, where
they witnessed firsthand, Cree workers operating their own
drill rigs approximately 700 meters below ground.

Under the Authorization of Impact-Causing Exploration
Work initiative, the sectoral team developed a standardized response letter for all exploration permit requests
submitted within Eeyou Istchee. This tool is intended to
provide a consistent and formalized means of communication with industry stakeholders while clearly reinforcing
Cree priorities. In particular, the letter recognizes the
importance of traditional activities and emphasizes the
protection of water, land, and wildlife. By establishing a
more permanent approach to these communications, the
team has strengthened how these concerns are conveyed
throughout the exploration authorization process.

The Cree Entrepreneurship Assistance Fund (CEAF) provides grants for new Cree business start-ups and funding
for existing Cree businesses to expand, modernize, and
upgrade their equipment and operations. The program
supports the development of Cree-owned local enterprises
and contributes to strengthening economic activity across
Eeyou Istchee.
Throughout the 2025–2026 fiscal year, the CEAF program
continued to respond to demand from entrepreneurs
across multiple sectors, including transport, construction,
and small service-based businesses. These investments
support the creation of local services, build business
capacity, and contribute to the circulation of funds within
Cree communities.

This year, the department made significant progress in

proper documentation, and closing files that had reached
their final stage. In total, ten (10) projects from previous
years were successfully closed out during the fiscal year,
reducing the number of outstanding files and improving
overall program management.

The program also included several ongoing multi-year
co-contribution projects, including the Elders Matching Contribution (MAMH Québec), Cree Social Economy

During the same period, four (4) new projects were approved under CEAF, supporting continued investment in
Cree entrepreneurship and local economic development.

Looking ahead, the future of the RDF remains under discussion, as its funding framework tied to the Cree–Quebec

negotiation.

Overall, CEAF continues to play an important role in supporting Cree entrepreneurs while ensuring that program
delivery remains efficient, accountable, and aligned with
current priorities.

Regional Development Fund (RDF)
The Regional Development Fund (RDF) continued to
support economic diversification and strategic regional
development across Eeyou Istchee during the 2025–2026
fiscal year. Through targeted investments, the program
contributed to advancing infrastructure, communications,
and economic initiatives that support long-term growth
and capacity-building.
Throughout the year, several projects progressed through
approval, implementation, and completion stages. Major
initiatives included the Eeyou Communications Network
(Meskino Project), the Waswanipi Hotel project, and the
Ouje-Bougoumou Commercial Center, which continued to
advance through funding and agreement processes. Additional projects approved during the year include the Eenatuk Cutting Team and the Mistissini Fish Tail Bay Project.
Several projects were also completed or moved into final
payment stages during the fiscal year, including the JBCCS
Cree TV Studio project, the COTA Alternative Lodging project, and the Chisasibi Telecommunications Radio Tower
project, reflecting continued progress in delivering tangible
economic outcomes through RDF funding.

Cree Social Economy Regional Table (CSERT)
The Department of Commerce and Industry oversees the Social Economy funding agreement between
the Cree Nation Government and the Government of
Quebec. The funding supports enterprises with social
aims, promoting traditional Cree activities and culture.
The Réseau des pôles d’économie sociale serves as the
primary resource hub for the social economy, with the
Chantier de l’économie sociale providing additional
support and resources. Eeyou Istchee is represented by
the “Cree pole,” which covers the nine Cree communities through the Cree Social Economy Regional Table.
Throughout the year, activities focused on collaboration, outreach, and capacity building. The Social
Economy Advisor attended key meetings, completed AI
training, and participated in conferences in Ottawa and
Montreal. Outreach included the Cree Nation Business
Summit in Mistissini and joint presentations with FNQLEDC in Waskaganish, while representatives from all
nine communities were invited to the Montreal conference to support regional collaboration and knowledge
sharing.
