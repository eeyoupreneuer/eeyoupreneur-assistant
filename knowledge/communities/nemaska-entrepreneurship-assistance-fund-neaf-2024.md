1. PREAMBLE
1.1

General Preamble

Local Entrepreneurs (LE) provide services that enhance the prosperity of the Nemaska Eenouch. They
stimulate innovation, create wealth, provide employment opportunities, and build capacity within the
community. Given these benefits, the Cree Nation of Nemaska aims to foster entrepreneurship through the
Nemaska Entrepreneurship Assistance Fund (NEAF).

1.2

Role and Composition

The NEAF is designed to support LE in all phases of their projects, be it pre-start-up, start-up, or expansion.
Funding under the NEAF is subject to the funding-allocation policies of the Chief and Council of the Cree
Nation of Nemaska and other funding entities. All requests made by or for LE will be evaluated by the
Economic Development Officer (EDO) and the NEAF Committee.
The NEAF Committee may be asked to make recommendations to the Chief and Council regarding financial
requests that fall outside the guidelines. Its mandate also includes making recommendations that help
alleviate barriers to entry for community entrepreneurs.
The NEAF Committee consists of the Treasurer, the Councillor tasked with the Economic Development
Portfolio, as well as three (3) other members chosen by the Council of the Cree Nation of Nemaska.
The President and the Secretary of the NEAF Committee is appointed by the NEAF Committee every two
years or in the case of a vacancy. The President is the authorized signatory of official documents originating
from the NEAF. The Secretary must plan meetings, create the agenda, and record the decisions made by
the NEAF Committee.

3. ELIGIBILITY
LE may apply to the NEAF in two different capacities: as individuals (sole proprietorship) or as other
businesses.

3.1

Individuals (Sole Proprietorship)

To be eligible for funding, LE must meet the following criteria:
•

•
•

Be at least 18 years of age *1
Be a beneficiary of the Cree Nation of Nemaska
Have a physical address in Nemaska
Have a business project within the territory of Nemaska (Category 1A, 1B, 2 and 3 lands)
Develop a project in compliance with all laws and bylaws and not associated with the following
sectors: gambling, tobacco, cannabis, exploitative activities, and passive investments

12.

MINI-ENTREPRENEUR PROGRAM

The Mini-Entrepreneur Program provides a quick and straightforward financing solution for young entrepreneurs
who wish to build a small-scale business project. The program is geared for youth between 5 and 17 years of
age. No business plan is required for this grant.

12.1 Application Procedure
Local Mini-Entrepreneurs wishing to apply for funding must submit a letter of motivation explaining the project
and the types of anticipated expenses.

12.2 Financial Assistance
The NEAF will cover 100% of total eligible costs through a non-refundable grant up to a maximum amount of:
• $250 for members between the ages of 5 and 12 years
• $500 for members between the ages of 13 and 17 years
A mini-entrepreneur may only submit one (1) request for financial assistance per age bracket (i.e., once between
the ages of 5-12 years and once between the ages of 13-17 years).

12.3 Conditions
Financial assistance is subject to the following conditions:
1. The Mini-Entrepreneur must use the funds in accordance with the project
2. The Mini-Entrepreneur must provide a project debriefing to the EDO and/or the NEAF up to one (1)
year after receiving the grant
If LE should fail to meet any of these conditions, they may be required to return the grant in full.

14.

PAYMENT

Payment will be made directly to the third-party service providers or product suppliers rather than to the LE,
except for travel-related expenses.
No expense may be incurred until all funding sources are confirmed. In addition, all disbursement will have
to be approved by EDO and the Treasurer and could be contingent on the satisfactory completion of various
funding conditions. If the amount is left unused for more than one (1) year, the NEAF Committee has the
right to cancel the grant. In the event that the LE requires an extension, a written request must be made to
the NEAF Committee.

15.

PUBLIC DISCLOSURE

16.

EVALUATION PROCESS

Each project will be evaluated on the following criteria:
• Contribution to the economic development of the community
• Response to a specific need in the community
• Competitive advantage
• Long-term viability of the business
• Business plan
• Revenue forecast
• Qualification and experience of LE
• Experience and skilled labour
The LE’s standing with Cree Nation of Nemaska will be evaluated. Past grants, loan balances, rental arrears,
unpaid loans, defaulted loans, etc. from the Cree Nation of Nemaska or the Nemaska Development Corporation
will be assessed. In terms of rental arrears, the Committee will mainly evaluate the LE’s effort to repay. When
evaluating applications for the restructuring program, past loans, rental arrears and grants will be assessed, but
the Committee acknowledges that applicants are in a difficult financial situation and seeking assistance to
resolve it.
The NEAF Committee is authorized to review and refuse any quotes and offers of service from service
providers and consultants based on several key considerations, including their competence and track record,
costs, industry standards, strategic alignment quality, and other risk factors.

17.

APPEAL PROCESS

LE who are not satisfied with the analysis of their application may appeal the decision as follows:
• Submit a written letter to the EDO within 30 business days of receiving the NEAF Committee’s
decision, explaining their grievance and providing new information that may alter the decision.
• The NEAF Committee will review the new information and may reconsider its initial decision.
• A written response from the NEAF Committee will be sent to the LE within 30 business days of receipt of
the letter of appeal.
• In the event that the LE is still dissatisfied with the decision, they may appeal to the Chief and Council by
sending a letter and explaining their grievance.

18.

CONFLICTS OF INTEREST

The EDO, the Director of Land and Sustainable Development, the Chief, the Deputy Chief, the Director
General, the Deputy Director General and the members of the NEAF Committee are not eligible for financial
assistance. If a member of the NEAF Committee, the EDO or the Council has a financial interest in a project
or a direct family link with a funding applicant, he or she shall declare this conflict of interest and shall refrain
from voting on the funding decision.

19.

FUNDING LIMITS

LE who have received funding twice from a particular program must demonstrate their need for subsequent
funding to achieve their project. In addition, they must make a strong case for the added positive impact
that the project will make in the community, such as new employment opportunities, improved local
services, etc. Their past projects will be evaluated to determine whether they have the capacity to
successfully carry out a new business project.
